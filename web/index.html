Seen from orbit

The planet keeps
a record.

For decades, NASA satellites have logged every storm, every dry spell, every green-up on Earth. Almost none of it reaches the person who has to decide what to plant.

Uruguay

Río Negro, western Uruguay

Where one season
can undo years.

Rain-fed soybeans and corn, with no irrigation to fall back on. In 2022–23 the worst drought in generations dried these fields and swallowed much of the harvest. Here, the weather is not a headline. It is the whole year.

Real satellite imagery, all the way down to the paddocks.

Meet Alex · An illustrative story

Three paddocks.
One bet a year.

Every winter, Alex leaves this soil bare between soybean crops. Every winter, the rain carries a little more of the farm into the creek. Alex wants to try something different, but in farming you only get one try a year.

Soybeans→Bare winter→Soybeans

Alex is fictional; the paddocks are real. In juxtacrop the farmer draws the field and says what was planted. Seasons nobody remembers are rebuilt from satellite history.

Future one · The dry year

What if the
rain never comes?

The cover crop fails to establish. The soil cracks. The corn reaches for water that isn’t there. juxtacrop replays the plan against a drier season, with SMAP soil moisture and decades of IMERG rainfall, and marks the months where the crop is exposed. Before a single seed is bought.

☀Dry scenario Illustrative conditions

Future two · The wet year

And what if
it never stops?

Machinery sinks. The low spots flood. Bare soil washes downhill. Under wetter assumptions, juxtacrop flags drainage, field access and erosion paddock by paddock, and says plainly what it still doesn’t know, like soil depth.

≋Wet scenario Illustrative conditions

What juxtacrop does

Change the plan.
Not the farm.

Drag a crop onto a season and every indicator answers back: months of living cover, rain on bare soil, nitrogen, pests, water. Black oats through winter, then corn: the ground is never left naked. The trade-offs stay on the table, next to what Alex does today.

Soybeans→Black oat cover→Corn

Example informed by regional research from INIA Uruguay ↗. Indicators support a conversation with a local agronomist; they do not replace it.

Under the hood

Space gives context.
You give it meaning.

juxtacrop turns open NASA data into plain words for one field: when frost ends, when the soil dries out, when the land usually sits bare. Add what only the farmer knows and it becomes a decision, not a dataset.
